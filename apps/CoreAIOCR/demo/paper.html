On the Airspeed Velocity of Unladen Swallows:
Coconut Payload Capacity in African and European Hirundines

¹Round Table Institute of Avian Logistics, Camelot  ·  ²Cave of Caerbannog, Dept. of Dangerous Rabbits
Abstract. We address the foundational question raised at the Bridge of Death: whether a swallow may convey a coconut. Distinguishing African from European hirundines, we measure the unladen airspeed velocity at twenty-four miles per hour and show that a five-ounce bird cannot bear a one-pound coconut unless it grips the husk. Wingbeat frequency is held constant at forty-three beats per second. The Spanish Inquisition did not expect these results.

1. Introduction

For centuries, bridgekeepers have detained travellers with a single question of avian logistics. Prior work assumes a coconut is tropical and a swallow is not migratory, yet swallows do migrate. We resolve the paradox by treating the coconut as cargo and the swallow as a point mass beating its wings against gravity and ridicule alike.

2. Method

We model the bird as a point mass m beating at frequency f. With wing area S, air density ρ, and lift coefficient C, the steady airspeed velocity v that balances lift against the weight of bird plus coconut payload L follows from:

v = √( 2 ( L − m g ) / ( ρ S C ) ),   f = 43 s⁻¹.

To grip a coconut by the husk, the swallow must maintain non-negative lift margin. We reject the alternative hypothesis that two swallows carry it together on a line of twine, as no one was able to say "Ni" loudly enough to confirm it.

4. Conclusion

A single swallow cannot carry a coconut. It could, however, grip it by the husk. We thank the Knights Who Say "Ni" for the shrubbery and recommend counting strictly to three.
